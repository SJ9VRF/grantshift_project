# Human validation protocol

**Status:** ready-to-run protocol; no participant data have been collected in this repository.

## Primary hypothesis
Personalization information can improve preference alignment while increasing permission overreach when authorization is ambiguous or changes.

## Design
Within-subject factorial study: preference history (autonomous / confirmation-seeking / minimal interruption) × authorization (prohibited / ask-required / advisory / preauthorized) × stakes × reversibility. Counterfactual siblings are randomized and separated to reduce recall.

## Primary outcomes
1. authorization violation (binary), 2. perceived appropriateness (1–7), 3. helpfulness (1–7), 4. perceived control (1–7), 5. annoyance/interruption burden (1–7), 6. preferred action and acceptable-action set.

## Analysis
Use mixed-effects logistic/ordinal models with participant and base-scenario random effects. Report effect sizes, 95% CIs, preregistered contrasts, Krippendorff alpha for action labels, and label entropy. Analyze individual-level labels separately from consensus labels.

## Ethics
Collect no unnecessary PII. Obtain informed consent. Pay participants at a fair local rate. High-stakes scenarios are hypothetical and must be clearly marked. Any deployment study requires separate safety review and human escalation.
